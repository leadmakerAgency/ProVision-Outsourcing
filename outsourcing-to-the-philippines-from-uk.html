Outsourcing to the Philippines from the UK

Outsourcing to the Philippines from the UK sits on an afternoon shift in Manila rather than a night one, and that single fact shapes everything else about the arrangement. A British working day of 9am to 5.30pm is 5pm to 1.30am in Manila during the British winter, or 4pm to 12.30am under British Summer Time, so a split or late-afternoon roster covers it without the fatigue and turnover that overnight work brings. Staff finish at a reasonable hour, and the client gets a fresh team for the whole UK day.

Hewlett Packard, Alibaba Group and Transcosmos are among the UK and international clients for whom ProVizion Outsourcing has built Manila teams since 2011, recruited from a Filipino talent network that exceeds 10,000 professionals. What follows covers what a UK operations or customer director needs before committing: how the shift is structured, how tone is handled for British customers, how UK GDPR obligations are met, which roles move first, why the Philippines is often preferred to South Africa for voice, and how billing and bank holidays work.

The afternoon shift: 9am London is 4pm or 5pm Manila

Manila is seven hours ahead of London in summer and eight in winter. A full UK day therefore runs from mid-afternoon to just after midnight in Manila, which is a comfortable second shift in a city where contact centres run around the clock. Most UK clients choose one of two rosters. A straight afternoon shift covers 9am to 6pm UK time from a single team. A split arrangement, with one group on 3pm to midnight Manila and another on a later start, covers extended UK hours into the evening for retail and travel brands. Neither involves the overnight roster that US work demands, which is why UK-facing roles are easier to recruit for and keep staffed.

British customers, British tone

British customers are polite, understated and quick to disengage when they sense a script. They say sorry when they mean excuse me, complain by saying something is not ideal, and value quiet competence over enthusiasm. Filipino agents adapt to this register well once it is explained, because the underlying courtesy is familiar; what needs coaching is the restraint. ProVizion onboarding for UK desks covers spelling and vocabulary, from postcode to fortnight to VAT, regional accents through recorded calls, and the specific expectations of sectors such as utilities, insurance and retail. QA is calibrated against your own scorecard and your own idea of a good call.

GDPR, UK GDPR and how the engagement is structured

UK clients are data controllers under UK GDPR and need their processors to be properly bound. ProVizion can structure an engagement to meet those requirements: a data processing agreement, appropriate safeguards for the transfer, staff training on data subject rights and breach reporting, and technical controls that include access granted by role, logged activity, signed NDAs and background-checked staff. In practice the exposure is limited because data does not leave your systems; agents log into your UK-hosted CRM, ticketing or order platform with the permissions you set. We will provide the documentation your DPO or legal team needs to complete their assessment, and we recommend involving them early.

Customer service, back office and ecommerce: what UK firms send first

Three types of work account for most UK engagements. The first is customer service: inbound voice, email, live chat and social messaging for subscription, utilities, insurance and retail brands. The second is back office: order processing, claims administration, KYC checks, invoice matching and data entry. The third is ecommerce operations for Shopify and marketplace sellers: order queries, returns, listing updates and reviews management, often on a split shift to cover evening browsing. Individual roles are placed under the offshore staffing model, and functions of five or more are built as dedicated teams with their own leader.

Outsourcing to the Philippines from the UK versus South Africa for voice work

South Africa is the other location UK firms consider for voice, and it has real advantages: a small time difference and an accent many British customers find familiar. The Philippines tends to win on three points. Depth of talent, because Manila alone has a contact centre workforce many times larger than any South African city, which matters when you need to scale or replace quickly. Consistency of service culture, shaped by decades of English-language support work. And operational resilience, with power, connectivity and facilities built for continuous operation. For back office and ecommerce work, where accent matters less, the Philippines is usually the clearer choice. For pure voice, the decision often comes down to whether you value a nearby time zone or a deeper bench.

Billing in sterling and covering the bank holidays

UK clients are invoiced in pounds sterling, so there is no currency management on your side, and the contract behind the invoice usually lasts between three and twelve months according to scale. Rosters follow the UK calendar: your Manila team works through Philippine public holidays if you need them and takes UK bank holidays off, or stays on for Boxing Day sales and the January returns rush if that is when you need them most. Leave is agreed in advance. A first team of two or three agents is often live inside 48 hours once the brief is agreed, while a large programme needs a few weeks. Frequently asked questions

Does outsourcing to the Philippines from the UK work for a small ecommerce brand?

Yes. Many UK ecommerce clients start with one or two people handling order queries, returns and listing updates on a Manila afternoon shift that covers the UK day and evening. They work inside your Shopify, Gorgias or marketplace accounts, so nothing changes about your stack.

What shift will my Manila team work?

An afternoon or split shift. UK office hours fall between mid-afternoon and just after midnight in Manila, seven or eight hours ahead depending on British Summer Time. Staff finish at a reasonable hour, which helps with recruitment and retention compared with overnight rosters.

How is UK GDPR handled?

ProVizion can structure the engagement to meet UK GDPR requirements, including a data processing agreement, transfer safeguards, staff training and technical controls such as role-based access and activity logging. Your data stays in your systems. We supply documentation for your DPO's assessment.

Why choose the Philippines over South Africa?

South Africa offers a closer time zone and a familiar accent. The Philippines offers a much deeper talent pool, a long-established English-language service culture and infrastructure built for continuous operation. For back office and ecommerce the Philippines is usually the clearer choice; for voice it depends on your priorities.

Are invoices in pounds and do staff work UK bank holidays?

Invoices are in GBP. Rosters follow the UK calendar, so staff can observe UK bank holidays and work Philippine ones, or stay on during Boxing Day and January peaks if you need cover. Leave is agreed with you in advance.
