Offshoring and outsourcing are not the same decision

The two words get swapped freely, which causes confusion at contract stage. Outsourcing means paying another organisation to deliver a process for you, with the provider deciding who does the work and how. Offshoring, in the sense Australian buyers mostly mean, is employing dedicated staff overseas who report to you, with a local provider handling recruitment, payroll, workspace and compliance.

The practical difference shows up in three places. You interview and choose the people. You set their tasks and priorities each day. And when someone performs well, they stay on your account rather than being rotated to another client. All ten providers below operate primarily in this offshoring shape, although a few also offer managed services. Our offshore staffing page describes the model in more detail.

Why the Philippine working day lines up with Australia

Manila sits at UTC+8, two hours behind Australian Eastern Standard Time and three behind daylight time, and level with Perth. A Philippine day shift starting at seven in the morning overlaps almost entirely with a Sydney or Melbourne business day. Staff work normal daytime hours, which supports retention and makes it easy for Australian managers to run stand-ups, screen-share and give feedback in real time.

Providers that also serve US clients run separate night shifts for that market, so ask which shift your team would be on and whether the office is fully staffed with support functions during it. The outsourcing to the Philippines overview covers shift patterns for each client region.

Roles Australian firms send offshore most often

  • Bookkeeping and accounts. Bank reconciliations, payables, receivables and BAS preparation support, working in Xero, MYOB or QuickBooks under an Australian accountant's review.
  • Customer service and order support. Email, chat and phone for e-commerce, subscription and services businesses during Australian hours.
  • Mortgage and insurance broking support. Loan packaging, document collection and CRM updates for brokers.
  • Real estate administration. Listing uploads, tenant communication and trust accounting support for agencies.
  • Digital marketing and design. Paid media management, content, SEO tasks and graphic production.
  • Software and IT support. Developers, testers and helpdesk staff working inside the client's ticketing and repositories.

Roles that rely on face-to-face contact, physical site visits or Australian professional licensing are better kept onshore, with offshore staff supporting the licensed person rather than replacing them.

Questions Australian buyers should put to every provider

  1. Who is the legal employer of my staff, and what statutory benefits do they receive under Philippine law?
  2. Is the office in a PEZA zone or a standard commercial building, and what does that mean for continuity and power backup?
  3. How do you handle Australian public holidays versus Philippine public holidays for my team's roster?
  4. Do you have Australian-based account management, and in which time zone do escalations get answered?
  5. What is your policy if I want to take a staff member onshore or bring them to Australia for training?
  6. Which Australian clients in my sector can I speak with directly?

Question one matters more than it looks. Some smaller providers engage staff as contractors rather than employees, which shifts risk onto the individual and tends to hurt retention. Our engagement process page sets out how ProVizion answers each of these.

Employment, entitlements and the question Australian directors always ask

Australian directors often ask whether Fair Work obligations apply to offshore staff. In the usual offshoring model the provider, a Philippine company, is the employer, and Philippine labour law governs the relationship. Reputable providers pay statutory contributions, the thirteenth-month payment and leave entitlements, and many add private health cover on top. What you owe is set out in your contract with the provider, so check it for notice periods, replacement guarantees and what happens if you end the engagement early.

None of this is a substitute for advice from your own accountant or lawyer, particularly around how the arrangement is treated for tax and whether any Australian privacy obligations attach to data the team handles.

A realistic first ninety days for an Australian team

Weeks one and two are recruitment and interviews. A single admin or support hire can be live within 48 hours of signing, while specialised finance or technical roles take longer to shortlist. Weeks three to six are onboarding: system access, process walkthroughs and daily check-ins. By week twelve, a well-run engagement has settled into weekly reviews, documented procedures and a staff member who anticipates rather than waits. Providers that offer a team leader option can shorten this curve for firms without spare management time.

Frequently asked questions

Are the top offshoring companies Philippines-owned or Australian-owned, and does it matter?

Several on this list were founded by Australians and now operate from Philippine offices, others are Philippine-owned, and some are part of larger groups. Ownership matters less than where account management sits, who employs your staff, and whether leadership understands Australian business hours and expectations.

Can an Australian small business offshore just one person?

Yes. Most providers on this list accept single-seat engagements, and home-based models such as Remote Staff are designed for it. Agreements usually run for a few months to a year according to team size, and small teams can be working within days once systems access is arranged.

Will offshore staff work on Australian public holidays?

That is set by your roster with the provider. Teams usually follow the client's holiday calendar for Australian public holidays and observe a handful of major Philippine holidays such as Christmas and Holy Week, with the specifics written into the service agreement.

How is client data kept safe when staff are in Manila?

Staff work inside your own systems so data stays where it already lives. Providers layer on permission-based access, audit trails, confidentiality agreements and pre-employment screening, and an engagement can be arranged to satisfy frameworks such as PCI DSS or GDPR where an Australian business needs that.
