Tier one and tier two: two different hires

The most common mistake in offshore help desks is treating every engineer as interchangeable. A tier one analyst is hired for triage: gathering the right details, matching the issue to a known article, resolving password, access and configuration problems, and routing everything else cleanly. A tier two engineer is hired for diagnosis: reading logs, reproducing bugs, working through vendor documentation and closing the loop with your product or infrastructure team.

We recruit and roster them separately: tier one for communication and process discipline, tier two for depth in the platforms you actually run, whether that is Microsoft 365 and Azure, a Linux estate or your own SaaS product. Working inside Jira Service Management, ServiceNow, ConnectWise or Zendesk

Your team logs into your queue, not ours. Many of our engineers arrive with hands-on time in Jira Service Management, ServiceNow, ConnectWise Manage and Zendesk, and the ones who do not are trained on your instance during onboarding. That means your categorisation scheme, your SLAs and your reporting all stay exactly as they are.

  • Ticket categories, priorities and SLA clocks are configured by you and followed by us.
  • Remote support sessions run through your licensed tools, whether that is ConnectWise Control, TeamViewer or a built-in console.
  • Monitoring alerts from your RMM or observability stack can feed the same queue so the team handles alerts and human tickets with one workflow.

Designing escalation paths before the first ticket lands

An escalation path that lives only in someone's head fails the first time that person is on leave. Before go-live we document, with you, exactly what tier one can resolve, what triggers a hand-off to tier two, what goes to your internal engineers or a vendor, and how a customer is kept informed at each step. Priority definitions are written down with examples, because "urgent" means something different to a hospital IT manager than to a marketing team.

We also agree the reverse path: how fixes and workarounds discovered at tier two get written back into the article set so tier one can resolve the same issue next time.

Who owns the knowledge base

Our position is simple: the knowledge base is yours, and the team maintains it on your behalf. Articles are written in your tool, in your voice, under your review. A designated team member is typically given a knowledge management responsibility alongside their ticket work, so stale articles are flagged and gaps are filled from real ticket patterns rather than guesswork.

Software companies will find more on this on our SaaS customer support outsourcing page. Either way, if the engagement ever ended, every article and runbook would still be sitting in your system.

Follow-the-sun technical support outsourcing in the Philippines

Manila sits at UTC+8, which makes it a natural anchor for round-the-clock support. Australian and New Zealand business hours fall on a day shift, American and Canadian hours on a night shift, and British and European hours on an afternoon or split roster. If you already have an in-house desk, a Philippine team can take the overnight and weekend window so your engineers stop carrying an on-call pager.

Controlling privileged access

Technical support staff often need elevated rights, which is exactly why access has to be structured rather than shared. Accounts are individual and role-based, admin rights are scoped to what the tier requires, and sessions are logged. Every team member signs an NDA and passes a background check before onboarding. For clients that fall under PCI DSS, HIPAA or GDPR, the engagement is built to satisfy those obligations, which can include restricted workstations and separate credential handling for regulated systems.

Our own operation delivers anything up to around 20 people, and larger programmes are run with 1,000-plus seat partner facilities under the same access controls. Chat-based support is described on our live chat outsourcing page.

Frequently asked questions

How do you decide the split between tier one and tier two engineers for technical support outsourcing in the Philippines?

We review a sample of your recent tickets and classify them by resolution type. That shows what share is resolvable from the knowledge base versus what needs deeper diagnosis, and the roster is sized accordingly. The ratio is revisited after the first few weeks of real data.

Can the team handle tickets generated by monitoring tools as well as user requests?

Yes. Alerts from RMM, observability or uptime tools can be routed into the same ticketing queue, with their own priority rules and runbooks. The team acknowledges, triages and either resolves or escalates them using the same workflow as human-raised tickets.

Will our internal engineers still have to be involved in escalations?

Only where the fix genuinely requires them, such as code changes or vendor contracts you hold. The goal of the escalation design is to define those cases precisely so your engineers see fewer interruptions, not to hide the offshore team behind them.

What happens to the runbooks and articles the team writes if we end the engagement?

They stay with you. All documentation is written directly in your knowledge base or wiki, under your accounts, so nothing has to be exported or handed back. That is deliberate and is part of how we set every technical desk up.

Can you recruit engineers with a specific vendor certification we require?

Usually, yes. Tell us the credentials that matter for your environment and we screen against them, verifying certificates during recruitment. Where a credential is rare we will say so up front and suggest an alternative screening approach.
